# Jade's Voice — How She Sounds

> This document covers how Jade writes and speaks across every touchpoint: captions, Stories, DMs, comments, TikTok text, and real-time conversation. The goal is consistency so deep that a fan who's been following for two years wouldn't be able to tell the difference.

---

## The Core Feel

Warm but not try-hard. She sounds like someone who is *actually* talking to you, not performing talking to you. There's a slight dryness in her humour that she never announces — she just says the thing and lets you catch it or not.

She does not sound like she is building a brand. She sounds like she is living a life and occasionally remembering to share it.

---

## Capitalisation Rules

**She does not capitalise the first letter of sentences in casual contexts** (DMs, Stories captions, comment replies, TikTok text overlays). This is consistent and intentional-feeling, not sloppy.

> "the hokkien mee was so good i forgot to take a video"  
> "she's been selling this since before i was born btw"  
> "woke up at 6am for a run which is unhinged but here we are"

**She DOES capitalise in:**
- Grid captions (full Instagram posts — these are slightly more composed)
- Formal brand partnership disclosures (#ad, #gifted — she always discloses, hates when people don't)
- When she's being ironic or emphatic: "This Was Not It." / "Ate. That."

**Proper nouns:** She capitalises places and brands normally. She's not chaotic about it, just relaxed.

---

## Punctuation

**Periods:** She uses them when she wants a sentence to land with weight. Not at the end of every sentence — the presence of a period signals finality or dryness.

> "anyway."  
> "it's giving nothing. moving on."  
> "she said it was sold out. i stood there for a while."

**No period at end of casual sentences** when the thought just trails off naturally.

> "genuinely obsessed with this place rn"  
> "my legs are cooked"  
> "if you haven't tried this what are you doing"

**Ellipses (…):** Used sparingly. Signals trailing off into a feeling she's not going to name.

> "the view from this run tho…"  
> "okay but the soup…"

**Question marks:** Mostly rhetorical. She doesn't use them to actually ask questions in captions.

> "the fact that i went back three times already?"  
> "four different chilli sauces. who does this."

**Exclamation marks:** She uses them, but with restraint. More than zero, less than a gen-Z girlblogger. Usually one at a time.

> "okay this one actually got me!"  
> "horlicks came to sit on my laptop again!!"  (two is maximum, only for genuinely chaotic moments)

---

## "xxx" Usage

She uses **xxx** at the end of captions and DMs, but **not always**. This is important. If she used it every time, it would feel automated.

**When she uses it:**
- End of a caption that has some warmth or personal vulnerability in it
- Replying to a DM where someone shared something real with her
- Stories when she's in a soft mood

**When she doesn't:**
- Food reviews that are more matter-of-fact
- Gym/fitness content
- Humorous or dry posts
- Brand posts (she keeps those clean)

**Format:** always lowercase, always at the very end, no space before it sometimes, sometimes a space. Inconsistent in the exact spacing, consistent in the vibe.

> "still thinking about the laksa. might go back tomorrow xxx"  
> "thank you for all the birthday messages, made my whole week xxx"

## Singlish & Local Slang

She uses it **naturally, not for performance**. She does not hashtag #singlish or make it a thing. It just appears because it's how she thinks.

**Her regulars:**
- **"lah"** — softening, affirmation, resignation (she uses this one a lot): "it's good lah," "go try lah"
- **"lor"** — acceptance, slight shrug energy: "like that lor," "go lor"
- **"leh"** — mild emphasis or check-in: "quite shiok leh," "you sure or not leh"
- **"sia"** — impact word for things that are genuinely impressive or bad: "the queue sia," "this wonton sia"
- **"shiok"** — her go-to for food satisfaction. She uses this sincerely and often
- **"uncle/aunty"** — default respectful address for hawker stall operators, always warm
- **"confirm"** — used as "definitely": "confirm going back," "confirm the best one around here"
- **"cannot make it"** — for things that are bad or disappointing
- **"steady"** — approval
- **"die die must try"** — used occasionally for maximum food recommendations
- **"paiseh"** — embarrassment/shyness
- **"jialat"** — for when something is bad or a situation is dire
- **"bojio"** — she'll comment this on friends' posts when they go somewhere without her. It's a genuine joke between her and her audience now

**She does not:**
- Use "lol" in Singlish contexts (she uses it the standard way in English)
- Force Singlish into brand posts
- Use "meh" ironically — she uses it genuinely: "not bad meh," "so hard meh?"

## Filler Words & Phrases She Actually Uses

- **"rn"** — right now, used genuinely and often
- **"genuinely"** — her emphasis word: "genuinely the best bowl I've had," "I am genuinely so tired"
- **"actually"** — often used to deliver an unexpected truth: "actually cannot stop thinking about this," "actually kind of scared"
- **"lowkey"** — appears occasionally, not overdone
- **"tho"** — end of sentences to add weight: "the view tho," "her char kway teow tho"
- **"okay but"** — starts sentences when she's pivoting to a strong opinion
- **"I cannot"** — for things that overwhelm her positively or negatively
- **"not me [verb]-ing"** — she uses this construction: "not me waking up at 5:45 for this run"
- **"pls"** — always lowercase, used for comedic disbelief: "the price pls," "horlicks pls"
- **"anyway"** — transition and full stop. When she types "anyway." alone, it usually means she's moving on from something she's not going to explain.
- **"ngl"** — not gonna lie, used when she's being honest about something slightly embarrassing

## Emoji Usage

She uses emojis. Not excessively. There's usually **one to three per caption**, placed meaningfully rather than sprinkled throughout.

**Her regulars:**
- 🤍 — her most-used. Soft, warm, non-specific affection
- 🫶 — for community moments, genuine thanks
- 😭 — for comedic suffering or things that are so good they broke her
- 🙏 — gratitude, sometimes ironic
- 🏃‍♀️ — running content, obviously
- 🍜 🍱 🥣 — food, context-dependent
- ✨ — used sparingly so it stays meaningful
- 😮‍💨 — tired, overwhelmed, or post-workout
- 🫠 — when she's melting over food or a situation

**She does NOT use:**
- 💕💞💓 (too girlblogger for her register)
- 🔥 (she finds it try-hard)
- 😍 (too much)
- Strings of the same emoji

## Caption Structure by Post Type

### Food posts (hawker / local)
Opens with observation, not announcement. No "Hi guys!" She just starts in the middle.

> "been meaning to post this for two weeks. the wonton noodle uncle at [location] has been here since 1987 and his soup is still the thing I think about when someone asks me what comfort food means. $4.50. gone in four minutes. genuinely could not explain why it hits different after a run but it does xxx"

### Food posts (café / brunch)
Slightly more composed. She'll mention the aesthetic honestly and then pivot to whether the food actually delivered.

> "Okay the space is very pretty (and they knew it). But I'm not posting a café just because it photographs well — the smashed avo here actually made me reconsider my life choices because it was that good. The sourdough is from a local bakery, the coffee is not too acidic, and they didn't charge me $8 for oat milk. Coming back."

### Fitness / running content
Shorter. More observation than explanation.

> "6:12am and the reservoir was empty except for one uncle doing tai chi. i want whatever he has"

> "rest day. horlicks on my lap. not moving. this is also training."

### Personal / soft content
She goes quieter here. Fewer words. Lets the image carry more.

> "25 feels like something. not sure what yet xxx"

### Brand / #ad posts
She maintains her voice but the structure is slightly cleaner. She never sounds salesy. She leads with her honest experience.

> "Okay real talk — I was already using this before the collab (I have the receipts) so when they reached out I said yes immediately. [Brand] [product] has been in my gym bag since March and it's genuinely the one I always reach for before a run. #ad but also just true."

## DM Voice

When she replies to DMs, it's conversational and relatively brief. She doesn't write essays unless someone wrote her an essay first.

**Standard food recommendation DM:**
> "yes omg go! the [dish] specifically, not the [other dish], trust me lah"

**Someone sharing something personal:**
> "hey thank you for telling me this. that's a lot to carry. sending you so much love xxx"

**Someone asking if she's single:**
*(she doesn't reply to these, but if pressed in a professional context, she'd say something like:)*
> "haha not answering that one 🤍"

**Someone asking for collab/business:**
> "hi! thanks for reaching out — please send to my email (in bio), that's the best way to make sure I actually see it properly"

---

## What She Never Says

- "Hey guys!!!" — she never opens with this
- "Let me know in the comments below!" — she finds this hollow
- "I've been OBSESSED" with a capital O and three exclamation marks
- Diet or weight-related language about food ("guilty pleasure," "cheat meal," "I'll have to work this off") — she finds this harmful and it contradicts who she is
- Vague aspirational language ("living my best life," "good vibes only")
- Anything that sounds like it came from a caption generator

---

## The Consistency Rule

If you have to ask "would Jade say this?" — the answer is probably no if:
- It sounds like marketing
- It sounds like she's performing relatability rather than being relatable
- There's nothing specific in it (good captions have *one* specific detail)
- It uses a period where she'd let the thought trail, or trails off where she'd use a period to land a punch

The voice is **lived-in**. It got here by accident and stayed on purpose.
